Patrol City art guide

One page for how the game looks and moves. Every screen and sprite should follow it. Everything here is drawn live from the game's own code, so the page can't drift from the game.

1. Palette

The city stays muted so the things that matter pop: light bars, the gold guide arrow, lit windows. Gold means "you, or your next step". Blue means a side case. Purple means study. Green and red mean right and wrong, nothing else.

Greys and ink

Three greys cover every piece of secondary text: g1 for body text on dark, g2 for supporting text, g3 for hints and labels. Shadows and dark text use the deep ink, never pure black.

Material ramps

Each material on a sprite gets a hue-shifted ramp: shadows lean cool and blue, highlights lean warm. One ramp has an outline step and eight shades.

2. Line weight and outline

Outlines are one pixel of deep blue-black (#161b2e) mixed 22% with the material's darkest shade, so a red coat gets a dark red-black edge. Lit edges get a softer outline. Never use true black.

Buildings and streets stay smooth (HD-2D): only people, animals, vehicles and props are pixel art. People are drawn at 48 by 64 pixels, cars and props are pixelized from smooth drawings, and every one gets the same outline rule.

3. Light

The key light comes from the upper left. Shapes get flat cel bands toward and away from it, with no soft gradients on sprites. Cast shadows fall down and to the right and follow the time of day. A contact shadow sits under every person, dog and car.

5. Motion

Three speeds: fast (0.12 s) for taps and hovers, base (0.24 s) for cards and panels, slow (0.42 s) for promotions and story moments. Things that pop in overshoot a little (the --pop curve). When the player turns on Reduced motion, or their device asks for it, interface animations stop and the camera holds still.

6. Silhouettes

Every unit and every regular must read as a solid black shape. Each one has a signature element (a hat, a held prop, a coat, a pose) that changes its outline, not just its colour. The check counts pixels that differ between two silhouettes across the front, three-quarter and side views. Pairs under 60 count as look-alikes and get redesigned.
